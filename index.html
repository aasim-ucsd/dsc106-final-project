How Hot Will It Get?

Two climate futures, 1850–2100

Muska Mesdaq · Ayesha Asim · Ryan Kasem

A child born today could live through a world that is less than 1 °C warmer than their birth year, or one that is about 4.5 °C warmer. Which one depends on the choices we make about emissions.

This page uses CMIP6 climate model projections to compare two possible futures. Under SSP1-2.6, emissions fall sharply and warming levels off. Under SSP5-8.5, emissions stay very high and warming keeps speeding up. Both futures share the same past. Scroll down to see where they split, and how much that split could matter to you.

Initial Prototype Write-up

1. What have you done so far?

So far, our group has continued building on our Project 3 interactive visualization, which uses CMIP6 climate model data to explore how global temperatures could change under different emissions scenarios. For our final project, we decided to narrow our focus to SSP1-2.6 and SSP5-8.5 to compare how lower and very high emissions could lead to different climate futures. We created six static visualizations for our proposal, including warming stripes, temperature threshold crossings, warming rates, climate model comparisons, a birth-year comparison, and model uncertainty. We also enhanced our website by adding our final project title, an introduction explaining the purpose of our project, and placeholders for all six visualizations to establish the overall structure of our webpage. Along with these changes, we planned interactive features such as hover tooltips, an adjustable temperature threshold, and a birth-year selector to make the data more engaging and easier to understand. For this initial prototype, we're also adapting our existing Project 3 visualization to focus on the two selected scenarios while keeping its interactive features.

2. What will be the most challenging part of your project to design, and why?

We think the most challenging part of our project will be turning our six visualizations into one interactive webpage that tells a clear and connected story. Since we're working with multiple climate models and two different emissions scenarios, we want to make sure the information is accurate without overwhelming users with too many graphs or interactions. Another challenge will be implementing features like the birth-year selector and adjustable temperature thresholds because the visualizations will need to update correctly based on user input. We also want to find a clear way to show uncertainty between climate models so readers understand that the projections aren't exact predictions. Although we've already established the basic structure of our website with an introduction and placeholders, we still need to figure out how to connect all the visualizations and make the transitions between them feel natural. Overall, our goal is to make the webpage interactive and informative while keeping it easy for users to follow.

Data: CMIP6 global surface air temperature anomalies, model ensemble means (Priestley Centre). Built with D3.js for DSC 106.
